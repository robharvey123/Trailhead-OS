## Priority Issues

### [P0] Tokenised client reports are publicly indexable
`curl /report/some-invalid-token` → **200** with `<meta name="robots" content="index, follow">`. `robots.txt` disallows only `/api/`, `/_next/`, `/admin` — **not** `/report/`. Independently verified in the parent context.

**Why it matters:** these are private client sell-in/sell-out reports. Any token URL that leaks via a referrer header, a shared link, or a crawled email is eligible for the Google index. This is a confidentiality issue, not an SEO nit — and the only P0 here with legal consequences.

**Fix:** export `robots: { index: false, follow: false }` from the route and add `Disallow: /report/` and `Disallow: /discovery` to `app/robots.ts`.

### [P0] Every social card on every page points at a 404
`/opengraph-image.png` → **404**. `/logo.png` → **404**. The rendered `og:image` on the landing page is `https://www.trailheadholdings.uk/opengraph-image.png`. All three verified in the parent context.

`app/opengraph-image.tsx` exists and would work, but the explicit `metadata.openGraph.images` in `app/layout.tsx:43,49` and `lib/seo.ts:61` overrides the file convention and points at a path that does not exist. `components/JsonLd.tsx:25,82` reference `${SITE_URL}/logo.png`; `public/` contains only SVGs.

**Why it matters:** every LinkedIn, X, Slack and WhatsApp share of every page renders with no preview image. For a consultancy whose inbound is largely referral and social, this is the cheapest possible win being left on the floor. It also fails Google's rich-result validation for both `Organization` and `BlogPosting`.

**Fix:** delete the explicit `openGraph.images` override so the file convention takes effect; add a real `public/logo.png`.

### [P0] There is no mobile navigation
`MarketingShell.tsx:81` wraps the entire nav *and* the Log in button in `className="hidden items-center gap-8 md:flex"`. Grep for `md:hidden`, `aria-expanded`, `hamburger` across `components/marketing` returns nothing. Verified visually at 375×812: **the header is the logo and nothing else.**

The footer offers only email, phone, Privacy, Terms and Contact — no product or case-study links.

**Why it matters:** on mobile, `/web-app-design` is unreachable. That is the page holding every named client, every price, every outcome and the FAQ. The site's entire proof layer is desktop-only by accident.

**Fix:** hamburger + slide-over with the six nav items and a full-width CTA; add a product column to the footer.

### [P1] 63% of the landing page ships invisible, including every CTA's destination
`app/globals.css:54` sets `.marketing-reveal { opacity: 0 }`, revealed only by `IntersectionObserver` in `Reveal.tsx`. Verified in the SSR HTML: **7 `marketing-reveal`, 0 `is-visible`, 0 `<noscript>`.**

All fifteen CTAs across the site resolve to `/#contact`, which is inside a Reveal block. Assessment A measured the live page at that anchor: `scrollY: 10141`, section `top: 0`, `is-visible: false`, `opacity: "0"` — a blank white viewport at the destination of every call to action. The section also has no `scroll-margin-top`, so once revealed its eyebrow sits under the 71px sticky header — while `web-app-design/page.tsx:421` applies `scroll-mt-24` correctly.

**Mitigation already present:** `globals.css:140-145` correctly disables the effect under `prefers-reduced-motion`. The gap is JS-off and slow-connection specifically.

**Fix:** invert the default — ship visible, add the animation class on mount. Add `scroll-mt-24` to both anchors. Consider routing CTAs to the standalone `/contact` page, which already renders the same form with no reveal race.

### [P1] 18 measured AA contrast failures, 12 from one palette decision
Assessment B parsed the compiled Tailwind CSS and walked 956 text nodes across 10 documents: 93 distinct pairs, 75 pass, 18 fail. sky-500 / sky-600 / orange-500 as foreground or CTA fill accounts for **12 of the 18, across 7 of 9 routes**.

Independently measured on the live page: primary CTA white on `#00a6f4` = **2.71:1**; secondary white on `#ff6900` = **2.89:1**. (Assessment B's static figures differ by <0.15 because Tailwind v4 emits an sRGB fallback plus a `lab()` upgrade; both agree, and no verdict changes.)

Also: `--marketing-border` `#E2E8F0` on white = **1.23:1**, below SC 1.4.11's 3:1 for every card and field border.

**Fix:** one change fixes most of it — shift accent text to sky-700 or darker and darken the CTA fills. Do not chase 18 individually.

### [P1] Titles are doubled on 5 routes, tripled on one
`lib/seo.ts:63` already appends `| Trailhead Holdings`, then `app/layout.tsx:27` applies `template: '%s | Trailhead Holdings'` on top.

- `/` → `… | Trailhead Holdings | Trailhead Holdings`
- `/contact` → the name appears **three times**
- `/mvp-cricket` → exports **no metadata at all**, so it inherits the site default and renders an 84-character title with no page-specific content

Four routes also canonicalise to the homepage (`/mvp-cricket`, `/privacy`, `/terms`, `/discovery`), actively telling Google they are duplicates of `/`. And `lib/seo.ts:4` uses `www.trailheadholdings.uk` while `lib/site.ts:24` links users to the apex.

**Fix:** single-point change at `lib/seo.ts:63`; add metadata to `/mvp-cricket`; reconcile the host.

### [P2] BrightFire is told three contradictory ways, suppressing the best proof point
`marketing/page.tsx:90` and `:754` and `bright-fire/page.tsx:90` all call it a demo/showcase. `web-app-design/page.tsx:143,152` says it was built for a real fire and security contractor in Harlow and "worked well enough that we productised it into Engineer OS and took it to market."

Meanwhile **Engineer OS — live, priced, with paying customers — appears nowhere on the landing page or in the nav.**

**Why it matters:** "we built this for a real contractor and it worked so well we turned it into a product now selling at £15/engineer/month" is the single most persuasive sentence this business has. It appears once, on the page with the weakest inbound link, and is contradicted on the three pages everyone lands on.

### [P2] The 17-step qualifying form is orphaned, all-mandatory, and off-brand
`app/(public)/discovery` serves a 17-step intake to any visitor. **No marketing page links to it.** All 17 steps are `required: true`, so the Skip control at `PublicDiscoveryForm.tsx:697` is dead code — including step 17, "How did you find us?", a marketing-attribution question made mandatory for the prospect. It renders in a terracotta/cream palette (85 raw hex literals, 39 distinct, zero tokens) unrelated to the marketing site.

Its inputs also have no label, no `id` and no `aria-label` — the rendered page has 2 inputs, 1 label, and that label belongs to the honeypot.

## What's Working

**1. `/web-app-design` is a genuinely excellent piece of persuasion.** Structured as argument, not gallery. The problem statements are written in the client's world: "When a client's insurer asked them to prove every alarm on a site had been serviced on time, assembling the evidence took most of a day." A fire-and-security operator recognises their own afternoon in that sentence. The format is falsifiable — named town, named firm, live URL, published price — which is exactly why a sceptic believes it.

**2. The consulting track record block (`marketing/page.tsx:125-161`) is checkable.** Five named companies with roles and dates, paired with the stat grid so "£5M+ revenue built from scratch" has a visible source rather than floating free. This is the correct relationship between a claim and its evidence — and the only place on the site where it exists.

**3. The engineering underneath is honest and careful.** Zero `href="#"`. Zero fabricated testimonials or client logos. Both forms POST to real endpoints with rate limiting, honeypots, field caps and HTML escaping — and the honeypots are correctly `aria-hidden` + off-screen rather than `display:none`, with the reasoning in a comment. Exactly one `<h1>` per route across all nine pages, zero heading-level skips. Fonts self-hosted with `display: swap`. The 962-line landing page is a server component; only three client components ship on the whole surface.
